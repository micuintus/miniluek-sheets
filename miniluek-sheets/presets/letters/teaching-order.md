# Teaching order of the letters lane

The letters lane teaches letters, first sounds, word beginnings and short printed words: six
plain sheets that teach, five sea-post sheets that review A2 to A6, and M7, the first picture-to-word
sheet. A1 has no revisit; children who know most letters start at A2. The evidence base is narrower than the
numbers lane's, and the rows in [research](research.md) carry evidence-type tags.

`letters.json` is English, `buchstaben.json` is German. Same sheets and structure, translated
titles, goals and course words.

## Number first

Letters teach best once the child can work a LÜK page: 12 numbered tasks, one tile per task,
pattern check at the end. Until the number sheets are mastered, they keep five days a week; on
the other two, a letters or a logic sheet is that day's sheet, so one sheet a day stays the cap.

The German lane is the reading route: school teaches reading in German, German is usually the
stronger language, and English basic decoding takes more than twice as long to establish
(Seymour et al. 2003, cited in [research](research.md)). The English lane is exposure: it
supports a second spoken language through shows, songs and one parent's time, and it does not
try to alphabetise in two codes at once, which no study has tested at ID-range scores.

## Where to start

Three minutes with the child before the first sheet:

1. **Same or different.** Write A and A, then A and B, on paper: "same or different?"
2. **Name the pictures.** Point at the twelve course pictures one by one ("CAT", "DOG", "FOX", "OTTER", "PIG", "GIFT", "PANDA", "MOUSE", "DAISY", "COOKIE", "BUNNY", "SUN"):
   the adult names each first, then the child repeats. A sheet only starts when the child knows
   the words well enough to repeat them.
3. **First sound.** "CAT starts with k. DOG starts with d. Show me the CAT."

| Result | Start with |
|---|---|
| cannot tell same from different letters | A1, letters only |
| knows most letters | A2 |
| names the letters and repeats the picture words | A3 |

The letters on A1, A2 and M2 are the first letters of the course words (German: B E F G H
K L M P S T W; English: B C D F G M O P S, plus A, E and H). The rest of the alphabet is not in the set; the
adult teaches letter names and sounds outside it, in the object step before A3.

## Before each sheet

Each sheet comes after the same idea spoken first: the adult says, claps, sings or points,
then the sheet. Start a sheet when the previous one is mastered, or when the condition below
holds.

| Sheet | First spoken | Start the sheet when |
|---|---|---|
| A1 same letter | letter cards face up: "find the A" | from the start |
| A2 uppercase to lowercase | magnetic letters: "big A, little a" | the child knows most uppercase letters |
| A3 first sound to picture | "CAT starts with k": show three pictures, the child points | A1 mastered or placed here by the check, and the picture words are known |
| A4 picture to first letter | "which letter does DOG start with?" | A3 mastered |
| A5 which part it starts with | say the start slowly: "CA… CAT" | A4 mastered (M4 is a review day, not a gate) |
| A6 read the word (the blue six hold the five-letter and longer words) | sound out CAT together, then the child alone | A5 mastered |
| M7 picture to word | name each picture, then read three words that differ in one letter: "CAT, CAP or HAT?" | A6 mastered and M6 done |

A sheet the child cannot do yet goes back until its condition holds. Keep the daily cap:
one sheet a day, reading work sits inside it, never on top of it.

## Sea-post sheets

M2 to M6 each take the day's slot a week after the plain sheet with their number is mastered, as
review, never as the daily sheet (A3 is reviewed by M3). A second plain-sheet check follows four weeks after mastery, as in the numbers lane. A1 has no sea-post revisit, so the four-week check is its only revisit. M7 runs the reverse direction of A6,
picture to word, with mostly new words: it is a daily sheet once A6 is mastered and M6 done.

| Sheet | After | Scene | Task | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 | A2 | little fish, big fish | a lowercase letter on a little fish | its capital |
| M3 | A3 | bottle post for the mermaid | a letter with its new word | the picture that starts with it |
| M4 | A4 | finds rising in bubbles | a new picture | its first letter |
| M5 | A5 | little fish blow word beginnings | the start of a new word | the picture that starts like that |
| M6 | A6 | the post boats arrive | a new word on a hull | its picture |
| M7 | A6 | parcels on the beach | a picture | its short word |

The six sheets tell one story, the mermaid's sea post, in the titles and the adult-read goal
lines. On M7 every first letter comes at least twice in a family (CAT/CAP, HAT/HEN, PIG/PEN;
BUS/BOX/BAT, CUP/COW/CAR; German BÄR/BUS, HUT/HAHN, OMA/OPA; TEE/TOR, WURM/WOLF, MAUS/MOND),
so the first letter alone never finds the answer. Several words carry long vowels, umlauts or
diphthongs (BÄR, HAHN, TEE, MAUS): with the picture named first, they are read as whole words
here. Letters and pictures are plain and uniform, each in its own fish, bottle, bubble, boat or
parcel, and no scene shows a letter outside the twelve stations. The plain sheets stay plain, so
the child learns the format first and the picture is the reward, not the teaching. Rich familiar
objects made preschool counting harder in one study, and decorated rooms held attention worse
in another (see [research](research.md)); the sea-post sheets are untested, so a child who
struggles on them goes back to the plain sheet.

## Design rules

The same rules as the numbers lane: one direction per sheet, two colour families on the two-pair
sheets, no answer copies another task (`check` refuses it), different-looking task and answer sides,
worked examples per family, feedback by pattern check. English letter names say the letter's
sound, not "see-ay-tee": the adult says the sounds ("kuh-ah-tuh") and slides them together.
German keeps its umlauts everywhere: letter cards, word-start cards, word pills and titles. Whole words on A6, M6 and M7 print as in books (Hund, Cat); single letters and fragments stay capitals. On the
initial-sound cards and on M3's bottles the picture word is captioned under the letter.

The twelve course words start with their letter's sound: CAT, DOG, FOX, OTTER, PIG, GIFT,
PANDA, MOUSE, DAISY, COOKIE, BUNNY, SUN (German: BÄR, KATZE, HUND, FUCHS, ENTE, MAUS,
GESCHENK, SONNE, TORTE, LUTSCHER, WURM, PILZ). The twelve sea-post words start with the same
letters' sounds and appear nowhere else: CRAB, DUCK, FISH, GOAT, PEAR, SEAL, CLOCK, DONKEY,
PIZZA, OCTOPUS, MONKEY, BANANA (German: FISCH, BIENE, KUH, WAL, HASE, LÖWE, MUSCHEL, SEEHUND,
TASSE, ERDBEERE, PANDA, GIRAFFE). Each picture has one name a child would give it, and no
second name taught first starts with another letter of the set (rabbit is taught as Hase, not Kaninchen; cup as Tasse, not Kaffee; the puppy would be "dog", the carrot
"Karotte"). Name each new picture together first, as on M7. German A4 tasks carry the German index
explicitly, because a picture file name would give the English letter (cat would ask for C
instead of K).
